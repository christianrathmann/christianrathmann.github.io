Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin

My research examines how sign languages are structured, used, learned, taught and mediated across multilingual Deaf communities and institutional contexts. I connect linguistic analysis with sociolinguistics, Deaf Studies, language policy and planning, interpreting and translation and sign language acquisition and learning. Moreover, I am a certified interpreter and translator.

Research

Research Areas

Six interconnected areas define my research across language structure, multilingualism, Deaf Studies, policy, interpreting / translation and education.

01

Sign Language Linguistics

Structure and meaning in sign languages, including argument structure, event structure (aspect and temporality), agreement, classifier constructions and corpus-based research.

02

Sign Language Sociolinguistics

Variation and change, multilingual Deaf communities and repertoires, language contact, language transmission and globalization, including the globalization of language and communication.

03

Deaf Studies

Diverse Deaf communities, cultural knowledge, cultural transmission and practices, community agency, institutional power in health, education and employment, and the social and ethical implications of AI and sign language technologies.

04

Language Policy & Planning

Language rights, language recognition and transmission, language planning (status planning, corpus planning, acquisition planning and modality planning) and language standardization.

05

Interpreting & Translation Studies

Interpreting, translation and mediation across sign languages, spoken languages and written languages.

06

Sign Language Acquisition, Learning, Teaching & Assessment

Sign language acquisition and learning across different learner profiles and educational contexts, including language literacy, language processing, curricula, teaching, CEFR-based approaches and language assessment.

Projects & Infrastructure

Research, Infrastructure & Collaboration

Selected long-term initiatives connect empirical research with sustainable infrastructures, capacity building, educational development and international collaboration.

Research infrastructure

DGS Corpus Project & Language Resources

Long-term corpus research and infrastructure for German Sign Language (DGS), together with SignBank 2.0 and VisuoLab, support linguistic and sociolinguistic research, language documentation and applications in education and interpreting.

ECML · Council of Europe

DeafSign

European collaboration on equitable and sustainable sign language learning opportunities, with a focus on multilingualism, educational access and teacher competences.

Interpreting and translation

DMIT

Research on Deaf mediators, interpreters and translators, including professional roles, education, recognition, employment and Deaf-led professionalization.

Tallinn University

EVKUR

Research and capacity building for Estonian Sign Language, connecting linguistic description, sociolinguistics, language resources, learning and teaching, assessment and language policy.
