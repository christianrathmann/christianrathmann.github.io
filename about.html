Christian Rathmann

About

A research profile connecting sign language linguistics, multilingualism, Deaf Studies, language rights, professional communication and education.

Personal research website · portrait used with permission.

I am a professor at the Department of Deaf Studies and Sign Language Interpreting at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin. My academic background is in linguistics, with a particular focus on sign languages and their structural, sociolinguistic, educational and societal dimensions.

My research spans six interconnected areas: sign language linguistics, sign language sociolinguistics, Deaf Studies, language policy and planning, interpreting and translation, and sign language learning, teaching and assessment. Across these areas, I am interested in the relationships between linguistic structure, language use, multilingual repertoires, Deaf Communities, institutions and language rights.

A central strand of my work concerns language variation and multilingualism in Deaf Communities. This includes research on language contact, globalization and International Sign Language (IntSL), which I approach as an additional language within multilingual Deaf ecologies.

My work in Deaf Studies builds on a Deaf-centered and community-centered understanding of the field. I am particularly interested in the relationships between sign languages, Deaf Communities, cultural knowledge, community agency, institutional power and individual and collective language rights.

In interpreting and translation studies, my research addresses interpreting, translation and mediation across sign languages, spoken languages and written languages. My research on sign language learning, teaching and assessment examines learner profiles, curricula, proficiency frameworks, teacher professionalization, language assessment and equitable access to learning opportunities.

Across my research, I aim to connect linguistic and interdisciplinary scholarship with questions that matter to sign language communities, educational institutions and public policy. This includes developing research infrastructures, educational resources and international collaborations that strengthen sign languages as languages of everyday communication, education, professional practice, research and public life.
