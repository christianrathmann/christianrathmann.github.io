Research

Four areas organize my work: sign language linguistics, Deaf Studies including language rights, Interpreting Studies, and research methods including language resources.

01
Structure · function · use · application

Sign Language Linguistics

I study sign languages as grammatical systems and social practice. Corpus-based and comparative work addresses argument structure, argument indexing, agreement, event structure, aspect and temporality. I also examine how findings on language form and use can inform education and language teaching.

Variation and language use

Sociolinguistic work examines variation, language contact and multilingual repertoires in Deaf communities, including mobility, digital communication and practices of International Sign (IS).

Acquisition, teaching and application

Early language access, acquisition, literacy and language processing connect with curricula, teacher education and resources, including ProSign, DeafSign and DGS storybooks for Deaf children.

02
Communities · rights

Deaf Studies

My work examines cultural knowledge, its transmission and Deaf community agency. It considers how education, healthcare and employment shape language access, representation and participation, and how Deaf people can participate in the development of sign language technologies and the governance of data.

Language rights and policy

Research addresses the recognition and institutional use of sign languages, status, corpus and acquisition planning, and the decision-making authority of language communities.

Deaf knowledge

Taubwissen documents Deaf language, culture and history. Research ethics includes participation, consent and the responsible use of identifiable video data.

03
Process · profession

Interpreting Studies

I study interpreting and translation within and across sign languages and between signed, spoken and written languages. Questions include decision-making, education, quality assessment and institutional conditions for accessible communication. Particular attention goes to Deaf interpreters, translators and mediators, their professional pathways and multilingual collaboration.

04
Methods · resources

Research Methods and Language Resources

Methodological work connects visual language data collection and annotation with corpus analysis, comparison and the development of reusable resources. Consent, data governance and responsible reuse matter especially where video data can identify signers.

Corpora and digital infrastructure

The Public DGS Corpus provides data for work on German Sign Language. SignBank 2.0 and VisuoLab, developed in the EVKUR context, support distinct lexicographic and multimodal research tasks.

DGS CorpusEVKUR

Language assessment

Assessment methods must respond to the visual modality. The German Sign Language Sentence Reproduction Test (DGS-SRT) makes video prompts, German translations, a scoring sheet and guidelines available.

DGS-SRT on GitHub

Connections

Research in context

The four areas inform one another: linguistic findings feed into education, language rights shape access and use, and interpreting research draws on multilingual repertoires. Research methods and language resources provide a shared empirical basis.
