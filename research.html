Christian Rathmann

Research

My research examines sign languages from structural, sociolinguistic, cultural, educational, translational and language-policy perspectives. The six areas below are analytically distinct but intentionally interconnected.

01
Structure

Sign Language Linguistics

My research on sign language structure focuses on grammar, morphosyntax, semantics and the interface between grammatical structure and signing space. Corpus-based work on German Sign Language (DGS) forms an important empirical basis.

Current themes

  • Grammar and morphosyntax
  • Argument structure and verb classes
  • Agreement and spatial reference
  • Temporality and aspect
  • Corpus-based sign language linguistics

Connected projects: DGS Corpus · EVKUR
Selected publications: Rathmann & Mathur (2002, 2011)

02
Variation & Multilingualism

Sign Language Sociolinguistics

I examine variation and change in sign languages, multilingual repertoires, language contact and globalization. A central strand concerns International Sign Language (IntSL) as an additional language within multilingual Deaf ecologies.

Current themes

  • Sociolinguistic variation and change
  • Multilingual Deaf Communities
  • Language contact
  • International Sign Language (IntSL)
  • Cross-signing and multilingual repertoires
  • Globalization and digital communication
  • Variation, stabilization and standardization

Connected projects: International Sign Language (IntSL) · EVKUR
Selected publications: Rathmann & Müller de Quadros (2022); Müller de Quadros & Rathmann (2026)

03

Deaf Studies

My work in Deaf Studies builds on a Deaf-centered understanding of the field in which sign languages, Deaf Communities, cultural knowledge, community governance and institutional power are interconnected. Particular attention is given to community agency and language rights.

Current themes

  • Deaf Studies as an academic field
  • Deaf Communities
  • Deaf-centered and Deaf-led research
  • Cultural knowledge and Deaf epistemologies
  • Community governance and representation
  • Language and minority rights
  • Institutional power and transformation

Connected projects: DMIT · DeafSign · Fachstelle Teilhabeberatung
Selected publications: Rathmann & Geissler (2020); Rathmann et al. (2024)

04

Language Policy & Planning

My research examines how sign languages are acquired, transmitted, recognized, maintained and institutionally supported. I focus on the right to language, individual and collective language rights, and language planning and standardization as community-related sociolinguistic processes.

Current themes

  • The right to language
  • Early and sustained access to sign languages
  • Individual and collective language rights
  • Language maintenance and transmission
  • Status, corpus and acquisition planning
  • Language recognition
  • Community-led language planning
  • Variation, stabilization and standardization

Connected projects: DeafSign · ProSign · EVKUR
Selected publications: Humphries et al. (2013, 2016); de Quadros & Rathmann (2021)

05

Interpreting & Translation

My research examines interpreting, translation and mediation across sign languages, spoken languages and written languages. A central strand concerns Deaf mediators, interpreters and translators, professionalization, education, quality and the institutional organization of professional communication.

Current themes

  • Sign language interpreting
  • Sign language translation
  • Mediation
  • Interpreter and translator education
  • Professional competence and assessment
  • Multilingual working practices
  • Deaf mediators, interpreters and translators
  • Professional recognition and employment
  • Quality and institutional responsibility
  • Community-centered professional practice

Connected projects: DMIT · Routledge Handbook · Fachstelle Teilhabeberatung
Selected publications: Stone, Adam, Müller de Quadros & Rathmann (eds., 2022)

06

Sign Language Learning, Teaching & Assessment

My research examines how sign languages are learned, taught and assessed across different learner profiles and educational contexts. CEFR-based work is combined with curriculum development, teacher professionalization, modality-appropriate assessment and equitable access to learning opportunities.

Current themes

  • First- and additional-language learning
  • Different learner and modality profiles
  • CEFR and sign languages
  • Curriculum development
  • Proficiency descriptors
  • Language assessment
  • Teacher education and professionalization
  • Teaching and learning resources
  • Educational policy
  • Equitable learning opportunities

Connected projects: ProSign · DeafSign · EVKUR
Selected publications: Leeson et al. (2016); Pärn et al. (2026)
