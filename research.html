Christian Rathmann

Research

My research examines sign languages from structural, sociolinguistic, cultural, educational, translational and language-policy perspectives. Six interconnected areas organize this work.

01
Structure & meaning

Sign Language Linguistics

My research in sign language linguistics examines structure and meaning in sign languages, with particular attention to argument structure, event structure, aspect and temporality, agreement, classifier constructions and corpus-based research. I am interested in the relationship between grammatical structure and its realization in the visual modality, including the use of signing space. Corpus research on German Sign Language (DGS) and comparative work across sign languages provide an important empirical basis.

DGS Corpus Project & language resources
02
Variation & multilingualism

Sign Language Sociolinguistics

My sociolinguistic research examines variation and change, multilingual Deaf communities and repertoires, language contact, language transmission and globalization, including the globalization of language and communication. I am particularly interested in how linguistic resources circulate across local, regional, national and transnational networks and how mobility, digital communication and institutional contexts affect language use. International Sign Language (IntSL), multilingual practices and language inequalities form important parts of this research.

03
Communities & institutions

Deaf Studies

My work in Deaf Studies examines diverse Deaf communities, cultural knowledge, cultural transmission and practices, community agency and institutional power in health, education and employment. I am particularly interested in how institutions shape language access, participation, representation and epistemic authority. My research also examines AI and sign language technologies, including linguistic diversity in training data, data governance, accessibility and the participation of Deaf communities in technological development.

Research ethics
04
Rights & planning

Language Policy & Planning

My research in language policy and planning examines language rights, language recognition, language transmission, language planning and language standardization. This includes status planning, corpus planning, acquisition planning and modality planning. I am particularly interested in the relationship between institutional policy and community agency and in approaches that support sign languages while maintaining linguistic diversity.

05
Professional communication

Interpreting & Translation Studies

My research in Interpreting and Translation Studies examines interpreting, translation and mediation across sign languages, spoken languages and written languages. I am interested in linguistic and professional processes as well as the education, assessment, recognition and professionalization of interpreters, translators and mediators. A particular focus concerns Deaf interpreters, translators and mediators, multilingual professional practice and the institutional conditions of accessible communication.

DMIT · Deaf Mediators, Interpreters and Translators
06
Acquisition, learning & assessment

Sign Language Acquisition, Learning, Teaching & Assessment

My research examines sign language acquisition and learning across different learner profiles and educational contexts. It connects language acquisition with literacy and reading competencies, multilingual language processing, curriculum development, teaching, CEFR-based approaches, teacher competences and language assessment.

Acquisition & Learning

Research on first- and additional-language acquisition examines age and conditions of acquisition, multilingual development, heritage signers and different learner profiles.

Language Literacy & Reading Competencies

My research examines relationships between sign language competence, written-language knowledge and reading development in Deaf learners and adults, including comparative work with Paul Miller, Tülin Kargin, Peter Hauser, Diane Clark and colleagues.

Language Processing & Cross-Language Co-activation

Research with Okan Kubus, Annette Villwock and Jill Morford has investigated cross-language co-activation between German Sign Language and written German during word recognition.

Learning, Teaching & Educational Resources

My work examines curricula, proficiency levels, communicative activities, teaching resources and teacher competences. ProSign, DeafSign, STEMSiL and Free Technology Signs connect this work with CEFR-based language education, STEM learning and multilingual educational resources.

ProSignDeafSignSTEMSiLFree Technology Signs

Storybooks for Deaf Children

In close collaboration with Chris Peters, I work on storybooks for Deaf children in German Sign Language (DGS). The work connects early language access, storytelling, literacy, reading development and cultural transmission through accessible sign language resources.

Language Assessment & DGS-SRT

My research on language assessment focuses on modality-appropriate approaches to measuring sign language proficiency. The German Sign Language Sentence Reproduction Test (DGS-SRT) provides openly available video stimuli, translations, scoring materials and assessment guidelines.

Connections

Connecting the Research Areas

These research areas are closely interconnected. Linguistic structure interacts with variation and multilingualism, language transmission connects sociolinguistics with policy and acquisition, interpreting and translation connect language research with professional practice and Deaf Studies brings questions of community agency, institutional power, research ethics and technological development into these relationships.
